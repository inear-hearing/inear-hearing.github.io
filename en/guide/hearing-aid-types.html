Hearing aid types at a glance — behind-the-ear, in-the-ear, rechargeable, battery

Guiin Hearing · Written 2026-10-24 · General guidance

Key points
  • Hearing aids are broadly behind-the-ear (worn behind the ear) or in-the-ear (placed in the ear canal).
  • Power is rechargeable or battery, with different care and accessories.
  • Neither is simply better. Hearing, ear shape, hand use and daily environment decide.
  • The style is chosen after checking hearing test results and ear condition.

Hearing aid names can be confusing at first. Here are the main types, explained by which daily life they suit. There is no ranking.

What types of hearing aids are there?

Behind-the-ear

  • The body sits behind the ear and sends sound to the ear canal through a thin tube or wire.
  • The body is outside the ear canal, so size is less constrained and it is often easier to learn to put in and take out.
  • It may overlap with glasses arms or mask straps — mention your habits at the consultation.

In-the-ear

  • Placed inside or at the entrance of the ear canal, usually custom-made from an ear impression (takes production time).
  • More affected by earwax and moisture; styles that close the ear canal can make your own voice sound boomy.
  • Ear canal shape or required power can make it hard to fit. See in-the-ear hearing aid checklist.

Rechargeable or battery — what is the difference?

  • Rechargeable: placed in a charger overnight. Keep the charger with you and the contacts clean.
  • Battery: small batteries are replaced by hand; keep spares.

Keep used and new batteries away from children and pets. If one is swallowed, go to the emergency room immediately.

What to mention at the consultation

  • Handling small items is difficult
  • Lots of earwax or often damp ears
  • Frequent or long phone calls
  • Always wearing glasses, a mask or a hat
  • Sweaty or outdoor work
  • Often away from home (remembering the charger)

See also 6 factors that change hearing aid prices.

Q. Which is better, behind-the-ear or in-the-ear?

A. There is no fixed ranking. Hearing, ear shape, hand use, earwax and moisture, and glasses or mask habits decide, after checking hearing test results and ear condition.

Q. How do rechargeable and battery hearing aids differ?

A. Rechargeable aids sit in a charger, so you need to carry the charger and keep the contacts clean; battery aids use small replaceable batteries, so handling and spare batteries matter.

This is general hearing aid guidance. The right style for you is decided after checking your hearing test results and ear condition.
