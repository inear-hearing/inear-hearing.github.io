Noise-induced hearing loss and workers’ compensation (산재): how do you get a hearing aid?

Guiin Hearing · Written 2026-10-24 · Based on Ministry of Government Legislation and COMWEL sources (accessed 24 Oct 2026)

Key points
  • Noise-induced hearing loss that meets the criteria can be covered by workers’ compensation insurance (COMWEL, 근로복지공단), including disability benefits and hearing aid support.
  • Criteria: sensorineural hearing loss of 40 dB or more in one ear after 3+ years of exposure to continuous noise of 85 dB(A) or more, plus other legal conditions.
  • You apply to COMWEL; after a special hearing examination at a medical institution designated by COMWEL, COMWEL decides.
  • This is a different system from the NHIS hearing aid benefit. Ask COMWEL (1588-0075) about what is provided in your case.

A COMWEL press release states that noise-induced hearing loss meeting certain criteria can receive disability benefits and hearing aid support through workers’ compensation insurance. Here are the steps based on official sources.

How do I get a hearing aid through workers’ compensation for noise-induced hearing loss?

  1. ① Check whether your case may meet the criteria (noise work period, hearing level)
  2. ② Apply to COMWEL with your work history records
  3. ③ Special hearing examination at a designated medical institution
  4. ④ COMWEL assesses work-relatedness and decides
  5. ⑤ Disability benefit and hearing aid support according to the decision → hearing aid consultation and fitting

When is noise-induced hearing loss recognized?

  • Exposure to continuous noise of 85 dB(A) or more for 3 years or more,
  • resulting in hearing loss of 40 dB or more in one ear,
  • as sensorineural hearing loss that also meets the other legal conditions (no obvious damage to the eardrum or middle ear; no air-bone gap on testing, with greater loss at high frequencies of 3,000 Hz and above; not caused by other factors such as age-related or sudden hearing loss, etc.).

Criteria can change. Check the latest criteria with COMWEL before applying; COMWEL decides each case based on work history and test results.

How do the application and special examination work?

  • Apply to COMWEL. Gather records showing where and how long you did noisy work; follow COMWEL’s guidance on documents.
  • COMWEL decides after a special hearing examination at a medical institution it designates. Follow COMWEL’s guidance (1588-0075) on where to take it.
  • A decision can take time, so prepare your documents early.

If approved, how is the hearing aid provided?

According to the National Rehabilitation Center’s guide to the workers’ compensation rehabilitation aids program, hearing aids are provided as rehabilitation aids under workers’ compensation insurance.

  • A specialist at a workers’ compensation medical institution prescribes and inspects the device.
  • It is provided when medical care ends, or during treatment if medically necessary.
  • After the service life passes, an additional device can be provided each time if still needed.
  • If you had to buy one yourself, the guide says to claim it from COMWEL as medical care expenses.

Ask COMWEL (1588-0075) about items and amounts. Guiin Hearing does not file claims or judge whether a claim will be approved.

How is this different from the NHIS hearing aid benefit?

  • NHIS benefit: for people registered with a hearing disability, under NHIS rules (standard amount KRW 1,310,000, 90% of the lower amount, 6-month prescription deadline, etc.).
  • Workers’ compensation: for people whose condition COMWEL recognizes as occupational, under COMWEL rules and procedures.
  • Whether both can apply, and in what order, depends on your case — ask each agency.

Q. Can I get a hearing aid through workers’ compensation for noise-induced hearing loss?

A. According to a COMWEL press release, noise-induced hearing loss meeting certain criteria can receive disability benefits and hearing aid support through workers’ compensation insurance. COMWEL decides each case.

Q. What are the recognition criteria?

A. Sensorineural hearing loss of 40 dB or more in one ear after 3 or more years of exposure to continuous noise of 85 dB(A) or more, meeting the other legal conditions as well. Check the latest criteria with COMWEL (1588-0075).

Sources
  • Ministry of Government Legislation, Easy Law — Criteria for occupational diseases: noise-induced hearing loss (Korean)
  • Korea Workers’ Compensation & Welfare Service press release, 28 June 2026 (Korean; KDI copy)
  • National Rehabilitation Center (Ministry of Health and Welfare) — Workers’ compensation rehabilitation aids program (Korean)

COMWEL decides recognition and benefits case by case. Check the latest criteria with COMWEL (1588-0075) before applying.
