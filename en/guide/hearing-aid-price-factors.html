Why do hearing aid prices differ? 6 factors to know before a consultation

Guiin Hearing · Written 2026-10-24 · General guidance (NHIS rules checked October 2026)

Key points
  • Hearing aid prices are not one fixed number; they depend on style, features, power type, one or both ears, and included items and aftercare.
  • No style or feature is simply “better”. What matters is whether it suits your hearing and daily life.
  • People registered with a hearing disability can receive the NHIS benefit. The standard amount is not a sales price.
  • At the consultation, ask what the quote includes (accessories, check-up and adjustment schedule).

Instead of quoting prices, this guide explains what changes the price. We give exact prices per product at the consultation, after checking your hearing and daily life.

How much does a hearing aid usually cost, and why is it hard to say?

Hearing, ear shape and daily life differ from person to person, so the same product may be fitted differently. These 6 factors make the difference.

6 factors that change the price

1. Style — behind-the-ear or in-the-ear

Behind-the-ear and in-the-ear styles are made differently; in-the-ear aids are usually custom-made from an ear impression. See hearing aid types at a glance.

2. Features — sound processing and adjustment range

The number of adjustable channels, processing in noisy places, situational programs and phone or TV connection all affect the price. The features you need depend on your daily life.

3. Power — rechargeable or battery

Rechargeable aids need a charger; battery aids use replaceable batteries. Care and included items differ.

4. One ear or both

The NHIS benefit covers one ear by default; both ears require meeting additional NHIS criteria.

5. Included items and aftercare

Check whether the charger, drying case and filters, and the number and timing of check-ups and adjustments, are included. Product warranty follows the manufacturer’s warranty.

6. Whether the NHIS benefit applies

The standard amount for a hearing aid is KRW 1,310,000 (5-year service life), and NHIS pays 90% of the standard amount or the actual price, whichever is lower (100% for certain near-poverty groups). The standard amount is not a sales price. Confirm your own amount with NHIS (1577-1000; foreign-language line 033-811-2000). See 5 steps to the hearing aid subsidy.

Questions to ask at the consultation

  • Which style and power type does this quote include?
  • Is it for one ear or both?
  • Which accessories are included?
  • When and how often are check-ups and adjustments after purchase?
  • Is this product covered by the NHIS benefit?

Q. What makes hearing aid prices differ?

A. Style (behind-the-ear or in-the-ear), sound processing features and adjustment range, rechargeable or battery, one or both ears, included items and aftercare, and whether the NHIS benefit applies. We give exact prices at the consultation.

Q. Is the NHIS standard amount of KRW 1,310,000 the price of a hearing aid?

A. No. It is a reference amount set by the program, not a sales price. NHIS pays 90% of the standard amount or the actual price, whichever is lower (100% for certain near-poverty groups).
