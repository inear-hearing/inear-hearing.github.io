Considering an In-the-Ear Hearing Aid? Tell Us About Your Daily Life First

Guiin Hearing · Written 2026-10-23 · Program details based on the NHIS website, October 2026

Key points
  • In-the-ear (ITE) hearing aids sit inside or at the entrance of the ear canal and are usually custom-made from an ear impression, so production takes time.
  • There is no single "right" style. What matters is your hearing, the shape of your ear and your daily life.
  • Please tell us about: hand dexterity, earwax and moisture, sensitivity to your own voice echoing, phone use, glasses and masks, sports and outdoor work.
  • This style may not suit narrow or very curved ear canals, hearing loss that needs high power, frequent ear infections, or children.

Many people tell us, "I'd like an in-the-ear hearing aid." We understand — it is less visible from the outside.

But no style is right for everyone. What matters more is whether it fits your hearing, the shape of your ear and your daily life. The same in-the-ear type can be comfortable for one person and a hassle to care for for another. So here is a list of things we'd like you to mention at the start of your consultation.

What is an in-the-ear hearing aid?

An in-the-ear hearing aid sits inside or at the entrance of the ear canal. Most are custom-made from an impression of your ear (귀본). The process is consultation → ear impression → production → fitting and adjustment, so production takes some time.

To take an ear impression, a small cotton or foam block is placed in the ear canal. If you have had a hole in your eardrum, ear surgery, or have an ear infection now, please tell us beforehand. If needed, your ear will first be checked at an ENT clinic.

Rather than thinking of behind-the-ear and in-the-ear types as "better" or "worse", think of it as choosing together the style that fits your life.

Please tell us first if any of these apply

1. Handling small objects is difficult

In-the-ear types are small, so putting them in, taking them out, changing batteries or charging can take time to get used to. If you have a hand tremor or stiff finger joints, please let us know. We will look together at sizes and options that are easier to handle.

2. You have a lot of earwax or your ears are often damp

Because it sits in the ear canal, an in-the-ear hearing aid is more affected by earwax and moisture. If the sound outlet gets blocked, the sound can become quieter. Tell us if you tend to have a lot of earwax or sweat a lot, and we will explain how to care for the device.

3. You are sensitive to your own voice echoing

The more a style blocks the ear canal, the more some people feel their own voice or chewing sounds echo (a "plugged" feeling). If this bothers you at first, it can often be reduced through adjustment and design — just tell us how it feels.

4. You make a lot of phone calls

Tell us how often you are on the phone and which ear you use. We will also decide whether you need a function to connect with your mobile phone. Features differ by model, so we check this at the consultation.

5. You always wear glasses or a mask

Behind-the-ear styles can get in the way of glasses arms and mask straps. If that is why you are considering an in-the-ear type, please say so. Whatever the style, we will look at what overlaps in your daily routine.

6. You often do sports, hiking or outdoor work

Let us know if you are often in sweat, rain or dust. We will explain storage and drying methods that suit your life.

When an in-the-ear type may not suit you

  • If your ear canal is narrow or strongly curved, it may be hard to make the size you want.
  • Depending on your degree of hearing loss, the power you need may be hard to achieve in a small device.
  • If you often have ear infections or discharge, please have your ears checked by an ENT specialist first.
  • For children, the ear canal changes shape as they grow, so the device may need to be remade.
  • If you hear low sounds well and only high sounds poorly, a style that blocks the ear canal may feel more "plugged".

We can give you a clear answer on these points after checking your hearing test results and ear condition.

Notes to prepare before your consultation

  • The situations where hearing is hardest (conversation, TV, phone, gatherings, etc.)
  • The hours of the day you want to wear a hearing aid
  • Whether using your hands is difficult (small buttons, small batteries)
  • Any history of earwax, moisture or ear infections
  • Your habits with glasses, masks or hats
  • Hearing test results (if you have them)

You don't need to decide on a style on the first day. We'll listen to how you live, let you try a demo unit if one is available, and you can take your time to decide.

If you are using the National Health Insurance benefit, we will check together whether the model you want is covered. For the process, see the 5 steps of the hearing aid subsidy.

This is general hearing aid guidance. The right style for you is decided after checking your hearing test results and ear condition.
