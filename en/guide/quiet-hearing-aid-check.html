Hearing Aid Sounds Quieter? 5 Things to Check at Home Before Bringing It In

Guiin Hearing · Written 2026-10-23 · Program details based on the NHIS website, October 2026

Key points
  • Check first: (1) power — battery or charging, (2) earwax and filters, (3) moisture, (4) volume and program settings, (5) your own ears.
  • Do not open the hearing aid or push sharp objects such as needles or cotton swabs into the sound outlet.
  • If one ear suddenly stops hearing well, or you also have dizziness or ringing (tinnitus), see an ENT doctor as soon as possible — before any hearing aid check.
  • If it is still quiet, book a visit and bring the hearing aid(s), the charger or batteries, and a note of the symptoms.

"I think my hearing aid is broken. It's gotten quiet."

When we get a call like this, we first ask a few questions — because when people bring the device in, quite often it is not broken but needs simple care. Here are five things you can check at home first. Most take only a few minutes.

Do not open the hearing aid yourself, and never push sharp objects such as needles or cotton swabs into the sound outlet. If the steps below don't help, just bring it in as it is.

1. Power — batteries and charging first

  • Battery models: try a new battery. Peel off the sticker and wait 1–2 minutes before inserting it. Check the direction (+ mark).
  • Rechargeable models: check that it was seated properly in the charger and that the charging light comes on. Also check the charging contacts for dust or earwax.

Keep used and new batteries out of reach of children and pets. If a battery is swallowed, go to an emergency room immediately (Korea emergency number 119).

2. The sound outlet — earwax and filters

This is one of the most common reasons a hearing aid sounds quieter.

  • Check the tip that goes into the ear (the dome; for in-the-ear types, the sound opening) for earwax.
  • Following the product manual: models with an earwax filter may have a blocked filter. If you have replacement filters, change them as the manual describes.
  • Clean gently with the brush provided or a dry cloth. Do not use water or alcohol.

If you don't remember how to change the filter, bring the device in and we'll show you.

3. Moisture — after sweat, rain or a shower

Sound can cut out or get quieter when the seasons change or after heavy sweating.

  • If you have a drying case or dryer, leave the hearing aid in it overnight.
  • For behind-the-ear models with a clear tube, check for water droplets inside the tube.
  • Do not dry it with a hair dryer, a microwave or in direct sunlight — this can damage it.

4. Settings — volume and programs

A button may have been pressed by mistake, lowering the volume or switching to another program (for example, a noisy-place program).

  • Turn the hearing aid off and on again. It depends on the model, but most return to the settings from your fitting.
  • If you use a phone app, check that the volume in the app hasn't been turned down.

5. Your own ears — do they feel blocked?

Sometimes the hearing aid is fine and the cause is in the ear.

  • A lot of earwax, or a blocked feeling after a cold, can make sounds seem quieter.
  • Don't dig deep into your ears with cotton swabs or ear picks.
  • In these cases, have your ears checked by an ENT specialist first.

In particular, if one ear suddenly stops hearing well — regardless of the hearing aid — or you also have dizziness or ringing in the ears, don't put it off: see an ENT doctor as soon as possible, before any hearing aid check.

Still quiet? Here's what to bring for a check-up

  • The hearing aid (both, if you wear two)
  • The charger or the batteries you were using
  • A note of when and in what situations it became quieter — e.g., "right side only, since a week ago", "since the day after I got caught in the rain"

If you call to book before coming, you won't have to wait.

Good everyday habits

  • Keep the hearing aid in a drying case at night.
  • Wipe the tip once each morning before putting it in.
  • If you bought it with the National Health Insurance benefit, get follow-up fitting care every year from 1 year after purchase (NHIS rule).
