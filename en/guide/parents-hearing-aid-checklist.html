Hearing aids for your parents: documents to bring and questions to ask

Guiin Hearing · Written 2026-10-24 · General guidance (NHIS rules checked October 2026)

Key points
  • Documents: ID, disability registration card (if registered), assistive device prescription (check the date), hearing test results, current hearing aids.
  • Ask your parents beforehand: where hearing is hardest, when they want to wear the aids, hand use, phone and glasses habits.
  • Ask at the consultation: style and care, check-up schedule, NHIS benefit eligibility and dates.
  • If hearing in one ear drops suddenly, or dizziness or tinnitus appears, do not delay — see an ENT doctor as soon as possible.

Many people start looking into hearing aids after noticing a parent turning up the TV or asking people to repeat themselves on the phone. Here is a one-page checklist.

What should we bring to a hearing aid consultation for a parent?

  • ID
  • Disability registration card (welfare card) — if registered with a hearing disability
  • Assistive device prescription — check the issue date (purchase within 6 months for the NHIS benefit)
  • Hearing test results
  • Current hearing aids with charger or batteries — if any
  • Notes on medications and any ear surgery or infections

Not yet registered? See hearing disability registration steps and 5 steps to the hearing aid subsidy.

Can an adult child buy a hearing aid for a parent?

A hearing aid needs the wearer’s hearing measurement, an ear check and fitting in person, so it is hard to choose and buy one on a parent’s behalf. Children can help by booking, coming along and noting the situations where hearing is hard.

What to ask your parents beforehand

  • Where is hearing hardest? (conversation, TV, phone, gatherings, outdoors)
  • When during the day do they want to wear hearing aids?
  • Is handling small items difficult? (batteries, small buttons)
  • Which ear do they use for the phone?
  • Do they always wear glasses, a mask or a hat?
  • What worries them? (appearance, care, cost)

Questions for the consultation

  • Which style suits my parent’s daily life, and how is it cared for?
  • When and how often are adjustments after the first fitting?
  • Is it covered by the NHIS benefit? How much time is left on the prescription?
  • How do we book the annual follow-up fitting from one year after purchase?

Tips for conversation

  • Face them and speak a little slowly and clearly — clear speech helps more than loud speech.
  • Turn down the TV and background noise.
  • If they miss something, rephrase rather than repeat.

When to see a doctor first

If hearing in one ear drops suddenly, or dizziness or tinnitus appears, do not delay — see an ENT doctor as soon as possible rather than a hearing aid consultation. Also see a doctor first if there is ear pain or discharge.

Q. What should we bring to a hearing aid consultation for a parent?

A. ID, the disability registration card (if registered), the assistive device prescription (check the issue date), hearing test results, and current hearing aids with charger or batteries.

Q. Can an adult child consult on behalf of a parent?

A. We recommend that the parent comes too, because the consultation includes hearing measurement, checking the ears and hearing directly about difficult situations.
